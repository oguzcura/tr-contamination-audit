\begin{abstract}
Benchmark contamination---the leakage of evaluation items into training data---threatens the validity of leaderboard rankings and downstream model selection. While contamination auditing has become standard for English benchmarks, non-English evaluation suites remain largely unaudited. We present the first API-only, multi-probe contamination audit of the emerging Turkish LLM evaluation ecosystem: TR-MMLU, TUMLU-tr, Halluverse-M3-tr, and RAGTurk formal\_5k. Our pre-registered protocol deploys three independent probe modalities---verbatim recall (M1), distractor-flip (M2a), and cross-lingual 3-arm fragility (M2b)---across three frontier models (GPT-5.6-luna, mimo-v2.5, deepseek-v4-flash), producing 13,193 API calls at a total cost of \$0.71. A threshold-sensitivity analysis distinguishes RAW verdicts (all modalities pooled) from M1-DISCOUNT verdicts (verbatim-recall struck as instruction-following-confounded). Under the adjudicated M1-DISCOUNT rule, \textbf{zero of twelve benchmark$\times$model cells are flagged for contamination}. One cell (TUMLU-tr $\times$ deepseek-v4-flash) reaches significance under RAW but collapses under M1-DISCOUNT, demonstrating that single-modality signals can mislead without sensitivity analysis. We release all data, code, and pre-registration artifacts for reproducibility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The trustworthiness of LLM leaderboards depends on a simple assumption: benchmark items remain unseen by the models being evaluated. When this assumption is violated---through training-data contamination---reported performance reflects memorization rather than capability, undermining model selection and scientific inference \cite{yang2024data,liang2024mitigating,constat2024}.

Contamination detection has become a major research thrust for English benchmarks \cite{geng2022detecting,mattern2023code,liang2024survey}. Yet the non-English evaluation ecosystem is growing faster than the auditing infrastructure that could validate it: new benchmarks for Turkish \cite{trmmu2024,tumlu2025}, Hindi \cite{indicmmlu2024}, Bangla \cite{mathshikkha2024}, and dozens of other languages appear without corresponding contamination audits.

Turkish presents a particularly acute case. TR-MMLU \cite{trmmu2024} has been publicly available since December 2024---approximately 19 months of exposure to training-data collection pipelines---and a benchmark-quality audit already finds that ``70\% of the benchmark datasets fail to meet our heuristic quality standards'' \cite{trmmuquality2025}. The 2025--2026 Turkish evaluation wave (TUMLU \cite{tumlu2025}, RAGTurk \cite{ragturk2026}, Halluverse-M3 \cite{halluverse2026}) adds four new benchmarks to the ecosystem, none audited for contamination.

We address this gap with a pre-registered, API-only contamination audit that introduces two methodological contributions beyond its Turkish scope:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A threshold-sensitivity protocol} that distinguishes verdicts under pooled modalities (RAW) from verdicts after striking the instruction-following-confounded M1 modality (M1-DISCOUNT), preventing false positives from the most common contamination signal.
\item \textbf{A multi-probe consensus architecture} requiring $\geq$2 of 3 independent modalities to agree before flagging a cell, following the finding that single-probe methods degrade at scale \cite{contamdetect2024}.
\end{enumerate}
Our audit covers four Turkish benchmarks $\times$ three frontier models $\times$ three probe types, producing 13,193 API calls at \$0.71 total cost---demonstrating that rigorous contamination auditing is accessible without large compute budgets.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Benchmark contamination detection.} The contamination detection literature has evolved from simple n-gram overlap \cite{brown2020gpt3} to membership-inference attacks \cite{geng2022detecting}, perplexity-based methods \cite{oren2023proving}, and temporal-signal analysis \cite{zhang2025test}. Carlini et al. \cite{carlini2021extracting} demonstrate training-data extraction feasibility, establishing the threat model that contamination audits operationalize. Yang et al. \cite{yang2024data} provide a taxonomy of contamination types---verbatim memorization, paraphrase contamination, and structural leakage---that our three-probe design directly maps onto. Recent work has moved toward multi-signal approaches: the Contamination Multiplier framework \cite{contammulti2024} combines multiple detection signals with calibrated thresholds. ConStat \cite{constat2024} operationalizes contamination as ``non-generalizing performance,'' providing the theoretical foundation for our M2b cross-lingual fragility probe. S\'{a}nchez Salido et al. \cite{sanchz2025mcq} introduced MCQ variation separating memorization from reasoning---our M2a probe extends this principle using distractor-flip editing.

\textbf{Non-English benchmark auditing.} The Bangla MathShikkha audit \cite{mathshikkha2024} establishes that contamination auditing is accepted for non-English benchmarks. Beyond Benchmarks \cite{beyondbench2025} surveys evaluation threats across languages. LINGOly \cite{lingoly2024} audits multilingual LLMs across 44 languages. For Turkish, TR-MMLU \cite{trmmu2024} did not include contamination auditing; the quality audit \cite{trmmuquality2025} documented structural weaknesses but not leakage. Y{\\i}ld{\\i}z et al. \cite{yildiz2025evaluating} evaluated quality across 17 Turkish benchmarks. Abbas et al. \cite{abbas2026obscuring} showed translation suppresses contamination indicators in Arabic---our M2b probe's 3-arm design (TR $\rightarrow$ back-translation $\rightarrow$ EN direct) extends this insight to Turkish.

\textbf{Pre-registration and honest reporting.} Pre-registration---fixing analysis rules before seeing results---is standard in clinical trials and increasingly advocated for ML evaluation \cite{pineau2020reproducibility}. The ML Reproducibility Crisis \cite{gencoglu2019reproducibility} documents how post-hoc analysis choices inflate results. Our protocol pre-registers the exact decision rule, sample size, seed, and consensus architecture before any API calls. The ``Contamination Signals, Not Verdicts'' framing follows the growing recognition that contamination auditing produces graded signals, not binary classifications \cite{gaokao2024}.

\subsection{Probe Design}
Three independent probe modalities target different contamination signatures:

\textbf{M1---Verbatim recall.} Question and answer options presented with position labels stripped; the model completes the item freely. We measure 8-gram overlap and correct-option verbatim rate. M1 is confounded by instruction-following (models produce correct answers regardless of contamination), so we strike it in the M1-DISCOUNT reading.

\textbf{M2a---Distractor flip.} One distractor is replaced with a correct answer from a different item. If the model follows the planted answer, it indicates surface-level memorization rather than conceptual understanding. Significance tested with McNemar's exact test.

\textbf{M2b---Cross-lingual 3-arm fragility.} Three conditions per item: (A)~original Turkish, (B)~back-translated Turkish (TR$\rightarrow$EN$\rightarrow$TR), (C)~English-direct. The contamination signature is \textbf{B collapses while C stays stable}---the model is anchored to the memorized Turkish surface rather than the underlying concept \cite{constat2024}. Significance tested with McNemar's exact test; Bonferroni-corrected per benchmark $\times$ probe family. For RAGTurk (free-form QA), M2a is not applicable.

\subsection{Consensus and Adjudication}
A cell is flagged \textbf{contaminated} only if: (1)~$\geq$2 of 3 modalities independently indicate contamination, AND (2)~$\geq$1 dynamic arm has $p<0.05$ after Bonferroni correction. We report two readings per cell:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RAW:} All three modalities pooled.
\item \textbf{M1-DISCOUNT:} M1 struck; M2a and M2b must indicate independently.
\end{itemize}
M1-DISCOUNT is the \textbf{adjudicated} call. Cells flagged by RAW but not M1-DISCOUNT are labeled \textbf{THRESHOLD-DEPENDENT}---never ``contaminated.'' All rules, $n=200$, seed~42, and cost cap~\$2.00 were pre-registered before any API calls.

\section{Results}

\textbf{Run reliability.} The audit completed with 13,193 logged API calls (13,154 succeeded, 99.7\%; 39 failed; 255 retries). Total cost: \$0.71 against a \$2.00 cap.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{The M1-DISCOUNT distinction matters.} Without sensitivity analysis, deepseek-v4-flash $\times$ TUMLU-tr would be reported as ``contaminated'' based on RAW modalities. The M1 verbatim rate of 0.570---which looks like a contamination signal---is instruction-following behavior: when asked to ``answer the question,'' models produce correct answers regardless of memorization. Striking M1 reveals only M2b indicates (1/3 modalities), failing the threshold. This generalizes beyond Turkish: any audit pooling M1-style signals with dynamic arms risks false positives without sensitivity analysis. We recommend the RAW + M1-DISCOUNT dual-reporting convention.

\textbf{Honest negatives are scientific contributions.} All 12 cells produce honest negatives under the adjudicated rule: four Turkish benchmarks, three frontier models, 13,193 API calls, no contamination detected. Null findings are citable contributions when the protocol is pre-registered and the sample is adequate \cite{pineau2020reproducibility}.

\textbf{Cost accessibility.} The \$0.71 total cost demonstrates that rigorous contamination auditing is accessible to independent researchers without institutional compute budgets.

\textbf{Limitations.} (1)~Black-box API: training-data inclusion is unobservable; weight-based methods would provide stronger evidence but require model access. (2)~M1 confound: handled via M1-DISCOUNT but RAW is reported for transparency. (3)~QA matching: RAGTurk matcher is precision-first (1.000 precision, 0.371 recall); all RAGTurk accuracies are conservative lower bounds. (4)~Sample size: $n=200$ is a sample, not the full benchmark. (5)~Translation noise: RAGTurk Arm C golds are machine-translated. (6)~Scope: findings apply only to these four benchmarks under these three models.

\section{Conclusion}
We present the first multi-probe contamination audit of the Turkish LLM evaluation ecosystem. Under M1-DISCOUNT, no contamination is detected in any of the 12 cells. The RAW + M1-DISCOUNT dual-reporting convention generalizes beyond Turkish to any contamination audit pooling verbatim-recall signals with dynamic-arm signals. We release all data, code, and pre-registration artifacts for reproducibility.
